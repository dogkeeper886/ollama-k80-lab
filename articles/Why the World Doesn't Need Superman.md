# Why the World Doesn't Need Superman

*By Lois Lane*

---

As the familiar red cape cuts through the night sky, a collective roar goes up from
the streets of Metropolis. We have grown accustomed to looking up. We have trained
ourselves to close our eyes in moments of absolute terror, crying out a single name,
secure in the knowledge that he will pierce the clouds to catch our falling world in
hands that can lift oceans.

But herein lies our quiet undoing: we are bleeding away our very instinct to
survive.

Superman's presence acts as a flawless, blinding umbrella that systematically
paralyzes the human will. When a civilization outstrips its own duty to
self-preservation in favor of a recurring miracle, it ceases to grow. The quiet
bravery of the firefighter, the relentless exhaustion of the surgeon, the simple
grace of a stranger reaching out in the dark—all of these become invisible, eclipsed
by the blinding radiance of the Last Son of Krypton. We are transforming into a
reckless, deeply complacent species. We indulge our worst failures because, in the
dark corners of our minds, we believe an omnipotent alien savior will always arrive
to clean up our ruins.

The truest cruelty of this arrangement is that Superman's invulnerable flesh can
deflect an intercontinental missile, but it cannot stop the slow poison of addiction
flooding our poorest neighborhoods. He can shatter an oncoming asteroid, but he
cannot dent systemic corruption, corporate greed, or the brutal realities of
structural inequality. The deepest agonies of this world have never been physical
disasters requiring brute force; they are moral, political, and institutional crises
that demand human skin in the game.

We owe him our profound gratitude for his kindness. Yet, human civilization cannot be
built upon the fragile scaffolding of dependency on a deity. Humanity must retain its
right to bleed when it falls, to look into the mirror of its own mistakes, and to
light its own fires when the night turns cold.

A truly mature world does not scan the clouds waiting for a god to descend. It looks
outward, across the street, ensuring that we learn to become each other's salvation.
